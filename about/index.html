About SadAge

A clothing and print project about living in a strange, tired, overproduced world — and still trying to make something human inside it.

SadAge is not here to pretend that clothes can fix the world.

It is here because the world is already full of clothes, images, systems, noise and sadness and some of it can still be reworked by human hands.

01

What is SadAge?

SadAge is a clothing brand, print and object project working with secondhand garments, original graphics, handmade printing, dyeing, reworking and digital tools.

We make one-of-one pieces, printed garments, small artifacts and visual experiments. Some things are made from existing clothes. Some are built from blank bodies.

02

Why SadAge?

Because the age we live in is sad in a very ordinary way.

People are more connected and more alone. The world produces too much and repairs too little. Rich people want to look poor. Poor people are forced to look rich. Algorithms decide what deserves attention. Every object becomes content. Every feeling becomes a product.

SadAge is a name for that condition.
Not a solution. A witness.

03

What we make

We work with secondhand garments, reworked vintage pieces, hand-dyed T-shirts, handmade graphics, small objects and limited drops.

Each piece is treated as an existing body, not as a blank commercial unit. Some garments are found. Some are damaged. Some are repaired. Some are printed until they become something else.

  • secondhand and reworked clothing
  • printed T-shirts and garments
  • one-of-one pieces
  • small artifacts and visual objects
  • print experiments
  • digital-to-physical graphics
04

How we work

Most SadAge pieces pass through human hands several times. We select garments, prepare graphics, test placements, print manually, dye, rework, damage, repair and document the result.

We are interested in the moment where a normal piece of clothing stops being neutral and starts carrying a sign.

05

Why secondhand?

Because the world does not need another endless stream of new clothes pretending to be necessary.

Secondhand garments already have a history. They are not clean in the commercial sense. They have traces, shapes, fading, previous owners, wrong proportions and strange dignity. We work with that instead of hiding it.
